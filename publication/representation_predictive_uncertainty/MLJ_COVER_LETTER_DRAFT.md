Please consider our manuscript, “Representation-Dependent Predictive Uncertainty in Autoregressive Language Models,” for publication as a regular research article in *Machine Learning*.

The paper investigates whether the predictive uncertainty of autoregressive language models changes when semantically matched procedural content is expressed in different representations. Across a frozen set of 100 procedural tasks, a teacher-forced full-trajectory evaluation finds a consistent natural-language-versus-Python entropy contrast in three model families. Additional representation transitions locate both replicating and non-replicating contrasts, restricting the scope of the claim rather than assuming a universal representation hierarchy.

We believe the work is relevant to the machine learning literature because it clarifies how representation can affect the interpretation of token-level uncertainty measurements while preserving a clear distinction between predictive concentration and solution correctness. The submission includes a separate MLJ Contribution Information Sheet and a versioned reproducibility package.
